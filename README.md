# spectral-drift
Philosophy: "Spectral Drift"
Invisible forces made visible through the mathematics of coincidence. Multiple wave sources radiate outward in all directions simultaneously — each front indifferent to the others, each carrying phase information encoded at birth. Where fronts converge and align, energy accumulates into luminous peaks. Where they cancel, darkness pools. The canvas becomes a frozen moment of something perpetually in motion: the accumulated history of interference.
What elevates this beyond mere geometry is turbulence. An atmospheric noise field — slowly evolving, never repeating — distorts the phase of every wavefront as it travels. Signals arrive altered by invisible drift. Coherent interference patterns shiver and breathe. The system oscillates between ordered crystalline symmetry and organic dissolution, each state beautiful in its own right.
The algorithm is painstakingly calibrated. Each emitter carries a subtle frequency detune (±4%), introducing slow heterodyne beating between source pairs — a phenomenon any RF engineer would recognize as the ghost signal beneath the signal. Amplitude falloff follows inverse distance, so nearer sources dominate gracefully rather than absolutely. The distortion field uses two phase-offset noise octaves for richer spatial complexity, computed with care from someone who has spent countless hours tuning these relationships.
The soul of this work lives in what cannot be seen: the emitter positions, seeded and fixed, determine everything. Change the seed and the geometry shifts entirely — new interference nodes bloom, new dark corridors open. The parameters control the physics; the seed controls the geography. Every variation is a different landscape of the same invisible phenomenon.

Each seed places wave sources at unique positions. Their circular wavefronts propagate outward and collide — where they align, warm amber peaks bloom; where they cancel, cool blue troughs pool; where they nearly cancel, darkness. A slowly-evolving Perlin noise field distorts the phase of every wavefront as it travels, making the geometry breathe and shift — what starts as crisp moiré fringes gradually melts into something more organic.
Each source also carries a slight frequency detune (±4%), so pairs beat against each other at their own rates — slow heterodyne pulses that roll across the field.
